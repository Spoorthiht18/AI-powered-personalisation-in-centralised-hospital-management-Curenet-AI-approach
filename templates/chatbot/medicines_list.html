Paracetamol (Acetaminophen)
Description

Paracetamol (Acetaminophen) is a commonly used over-the-counter pain reliever and fever reducer. It's effective for treating many types of pain including headaches, toothaches, backaches, and pain associated with colds and flu. It works by blocking the production of certain chemicals in the brain that signal pain and cause fever.

Common Uses
  • Relief of mild to moderate pain
  • Reduction of fever
  • Treatment of headaches and migraines
  • Management of pain associated with arthritis
  • Relief of cold and flu symptoms
Dosage and Administration

For adults and children 12 years and older: 500-1000 mg every 4-6 hours as needed, not exceeding 4000 mg in 24 hours.

For children under 12 years: Dosage is based on weight and age. Consult a healthcare professional.

Side Effects
Common
  • Nausea
  • Upset stomach
  • Headache
Serious
  • Liver damage (with overdose or long-term use)
  • Allergic reactions (rash, itching, swelling)
  • Decreased blood cell counts (rare)
Precautions
  • Do not exceed the recommended dose
  • Avoid alcohol while taking paracetamol as it can increase the risk of liver damage
  • Consult a doctor if you have liver disease, kidney disease, or alcohol dependence
  • Not recommended for long-term use without medical supervision
Drug Interactions
  • Warfarin (may increase risk of bleeding)
  • Certain anti-seizure medications
  • Isoniazid (used to treat tuberculosis)
  • Other medications containing paracetamol (risk of overdose)
Aspirin (Acetylsalicylic Acid)
Description

Aspirin is a nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) used to reduce pain, fever, and inflammation. It also has anti-platelet effects, which means it can help prevent blood clots, making it useful for preventing heart attacks and strokes in high-risk individuals.

Common Uses
  • Relief of pain and inflammation
  • Reduction of fever
  • Prevention of heart attacks and strokes
  • Management of arthritis and other inflammatory conditions
Dosage and Administration

For pain or fever: 325-650 mg every 4-6 hours as needed, not exceeding 4000 mg in 24 hours.

For heart attack or stroke prevention: 75-325 mg daily as advised by a healthcare professional.

Side Effects
Common
  • Upset stomach
  • Heartburn
  • Nausea
  • Increased bleeding time
Serious
  • Gastrointestinal bleeding
  • Allergic reactions
  • Reye's syndrome (in children with viral infections)
  • Tinnitus (ringing in ears) with high doses
Precautions
  • Not recommended for children under 12 with viral illnesses (risk of Reye's syndrome)
  • Use with caution in people with asthma, allergies, stomach ulcers, or bleeding disorders
  • Stop taking at least 7 days before scheduled surgery
  • Consult a doctor before taking if you have kidney disease, liver disease, or heart failure
Drug Interactions
  • Anticoagulants (increased risk of bleeding)
  • Other NSAIDs (increased risk of side effects)
  • ACE inhibitors (reduced effectiveness)
  • Methotrexate (increased toxicity)
  • Corticosteroids (increased risk of gastrointestinal bleeding)
